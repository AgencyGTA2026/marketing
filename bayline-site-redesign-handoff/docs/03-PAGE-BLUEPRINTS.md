# Page Blueprints

## Homepage

Status: built in `current-build/app/page.tsx`.

Purpose: explain Bayline quickly, introduce websites, conversion and automation, establish trust, and move visitors toward a free homepage review.

Do not add extra homepage sections unless they provide real proof or answer a recurring sales objection.

## Location page

Status: Whitby built in `current-build/app/locations/whitby`.

Recommended order:

1. Local hero and embedded short form
2. Nearby-area strip
3. Four relevant service rows
4. Unique local decision context
5. Three location-page principles or proof points
6. Local FAQs
7. Closing CTA back to the form

Each city needs unique context, nearby areas, emphasis and FAQs. Do not only replace the city name.

## Blog index `/blog`

Goal: make the writing feel useful and credible while helping visitors find topics without a generic card wall.

Recommended order:

1. Editorial hero: “Practical notes on websites, automation and growth.”
2. One featured article with a large title, short excerpt, category and date
3. Simple category controls: All, Web Design, Local SEO, Automation, Operations
4. Chronological article list using full-width rows
5. Optional pagination or “Load more” when required
6. Compact CTA to request a homepage review

Article rows should contain title, excerpt, category, date, reading time and optional thumbnail. Avoid three-column repeated cards.

## Blog article `/blog/[slug]`

Recommended order:

1. Category, date and reading time
2. Clear article title and useful summary
3. Optional hero image
4. Readable content column around `680–760px`
5. Table of contents only for long articles
6. Contextual links to relevant Bayline services and related articles
7. Author/company note
8. One restrained closing CTA

Preserve CMS rich text, headings, lists, images, tables, code blocks and embedded media. Do not inject promotional blocks between every section.

## Services index `/services`

Goal: help a business owner understand which Bayline capability fits their problem.

Recommended order:

1. Hero: one clear explanation of the combined offer
2. Six full-width service rows
3. “Start with the bottleneck” guidance section
4. Simple four-step process
5. Trust and ownership principles
6. Inquiry CTA or embedded form

Each service row needs:

- Service name
- Customer problem
- Practical outcome
- Three representative deliverables
- Link to the service detail page

## Service detail `/services/[slug]`

Use one reusable template while allowing service-specific sections.

Recommended order:

1. Service-specific hero and CTA
2. Who it is for
3. Problems it solves
4. Deliverables
5. Example workflow or process
6. Integration or technical considerations when relevant
7. FAQs
8. Related services
9. Short inquiry form with hidden service field

Do not lead with framework names. Put technical detail after the business outcome.

### Existing service routes

#### Custom websites

Emphasize clarity, trust, CMS control, performance, accessibility and ownership.

#### Landing pages

Emphasize one offer, one audience, one conversion path, ad relevance and tracking.

#### Web apps

Emphasize replacing spreadsheet friction, secure roles, portals, inventory and operational visibility.

#### Automation

Emphasize response time, CRM routing, email/SMS follow-up, notifications and fewer manual handoffs.

#### SEO

Emphasize technical foundations, useful local content, Google Business Profile alignment and honest expectations. Do not guarantee rankings.

#### Maintenance

Emphasize monitoring, backups, updates, support, ownership and predictable ongoing care.

## Contact `/contact`

Goal: make starting a conversation feel simple and low pressure.

Recommended desktop layout:

- Left: heading, short promise, email, phone, hours and scheduling option
- Right: complete inquiry form

Below the form:

1. Three-step “what happens next” explanation
2. Three or four concise FAQs
3. No duplicate giant sales CTA

Form fields:

- Name
- Work email
- Company, optional
- Website, optional
- Service interest
- Budget range, optional unless required for qualification
- Project note, optional
- Hidden source page, location, service, UTM values and referrer

## About and other future pages

Use the same editorial system. Prioritize real founder/company context, operating principles, relevant experience and how Bayline works. Avoid generic mission statements and invented team photography.
